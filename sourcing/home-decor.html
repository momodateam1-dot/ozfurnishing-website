Materials and construction

What it is made of
and what to ask for.

In decor, the material decides three things at once: how it looks, how it survives freight, and whether it can clear your market's compliance checks.

01

Ceramic & porcelain

The backbone of decor and tableware. Ask about glaze consistency and firing quality — colour drift between batches is the most common complaint.

↗
02

Glass

Mouth-blown, machine-pressed or tempered. Beautiful and fragile; packed properly it travels fine, packed cheaply it does not.

↗
03

Metal

Iron, brass, aluminium and stainless, with powder-coating, plating or hand-finished patina. Check coating adhesion on pieces headed for humid climates.

↗
04

Wood, bamboo & rattan

Mango wood, acacia, bamboo and natural rattan. Hand-finished pieces vary by batch, so approve a sample rather than a photograph.

↗
05

Textiles

Cotton, linen, polyester and blends. Specify weight, weave and colour fastness, especially for anything that will be washed.

↗
06

Resin & polyresin

Good for detailed shapes at low unit cost, and far more forgiving in transit than ceramic. Heavier than it looks, which affects freight.

Common questions

Before you send
the first enquiry.

These come up on nearly every decor order.

Can I buy just a few pieces?

Yes. Stocked decor can usually be bought one to five pieces at a time, and there is no minimum order value. The only thing to watch is freight: a single small parcel costs disproportionately more per item than a consolidated box, so most buyers add a few more pieces once they see the numbers.

How do you pack fragile items?

Fragile pieces are individually wrapped, boxed and cushioned, then placed inside the consolidated export carton with the heavy items at the bottom. Mirrors, large ceramics and glass get corner protection or a wooden crate where the value justifies it, and we photograph the packing before dispatch.

Can I mix items from different sellers?

Yes, and it is what most decor orders look like. We receive parcels from each seller into our Guangdong warehouse, check them against your list, hold them until everything has arrived and then pack one shipment with one tracking number.

Do tableware and candles need certification?

They can. Tableware sold for food use usually needs LFGB or FDA food-contact testing, and candles and fragrances have labelling rules in most markets. Tell us where the goods are going and we will confirm what applies before you order rather than after.
