Materials and construction

What it is made of
and where it fails.

Outdoor furniture fails predictably. Knowing which material fails how — and asking for the right rating up front — is the whole game.

01

PE rattan wicker

Synthetic wicker woven over an aluminium frame. Ask for UV-stabilised PE with a stated warranty; cheap PE chalk and splits after one summer.

↗
02

Aluminium

Powder-coated and rust-free, the safest frame for coastal and poolside use. Check coating thickness and that welds are ground smooth before coating.

↗
03

Teak & acacia

Naturally oily hardwoods that weather to silver if left untreated. Confirm whether the timber is plantation-grown and ask for documentation if your market requires it.

↗
04

Steel & wrought iron

Strong and heavy, but rusts the moment the coating is scratched. Fine inland, risky near salt water, and expensive to ship.

↗
05

Textilene & outdoor fabric

Sling fabric and cushion covers rated for UV exposure and water resistance. Solution-dyed acrylic lasts far longer than printed polyester.

↗
06

Glass & sintered tops

Tempered glass for table tops, sintered stone for heavier commercial settings. Both need proper crating to arrive intact.

Before you send the enquiry

Six things worth
deciding before you ask.

Outdoor briefs go wrong on environment more often than on product. Answer these and the quote will be accurate the first time.

Where the furniture will live: inland garden, coastal terrace, poolside or covered balcony
Whether it is for home use, rental turnover or commercial service, because it changes load and fabric ratings
Set configuration and how many seats you actually need, rather than how many look good in a photo
Frame material preference, and whether rust resistance matters more than weight
Cushion fabric expectation: shower-resistant, quick-dry or fully waterproof
Whether the pieces need to stack, fold or nest for storage in the off-season

Tell us the climate and the usage. A set specified for a dry courtyard will disappoint on a salt-water terrace, and we would rather say so now.

Common questions

Before you send
the first enquiry.

These are the questions that decide whether an outdoor order still looks good next year.

How long will outdoor furniture from China last?

It depends almost entirely on three choices: frame material, coating and fabric rating. Powder-coated aluminium with UV-stabilised PE rattan and solution-dyed acrylic cushions lasts many seasons, including near the coast. Untreated steel with cheap woven plastic and printed polyester cushions often does not survive one. We will tell you which is which before you buy.

Is the furniture waterproof or just weather-resistant?

Most outdoor furniture is weather-resistant rather than fully waterproof, which is a meaningful difference. Frames handle rain, but cushions need a quick-dry or solution-dyed fabric plus a cover if they will be left out. If you need furniture that stays outside uncovered all year, say so and we will specify it that way.

Why is shipping outdoor furniture so expensive?

Because you are shipping air as much as furniture. Sea freight on bulky goods is charged by volume, so a nested, flat-packed set costs far less to move than an assembled one. We measure the packed volume before booking and tell you the cubic metres, so you can see exactly what you are paying for.

Can I order a single set?

Yes. Stocked sets can usually be bought one at a time, and we will consolidate it with anything else you are buying. A single set does carry a higher freight cost per piece than a container, so if you are furnishing more than one area it is worth ordering them together.
