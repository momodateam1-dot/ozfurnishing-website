Materials and construction

What it is made of
and what to ask for.

Material drives three outcomes here: whether it is safe for food contact, whether it survives a dishwasher, and how much of it arrives unbroken.

01

Porcelain & stoneware

The standard for dinnerware. Porcelain is fired harder and chips less; stoneware is thicker and more relaxed. Confirm glaze consistency across batches.

↗
02

Bone china

Lighter and more translucent than porcelain, with a warm white tone. Popular for hotel and fine dining, and priced accordingly.

03

Glass

Soda-lime for everyday drinkware, borosilicate for heat resistance, lead-free crystal for premium ranges. Confirm dishwasher safety.

↗
04

Stainless steel

304 for cookware and cutlery that must resist corrosion, 430 for cheaper flatware. Ask which grade is quoted, because the difference is invisible.

05

Cast iron & carbon steel

Enamelled or pre-seasoned. Heavy, durable and expensive to ship; worth consolidating with a larger order rather than sending alone.

Utensils, boards and storage. Confirm food-grade silicone and that any plastic is BPA-free if your market requires it.

Bulk packed vs. properly specified

What changes
when the packing is written down.

Tableware failures are almost never about the product. They are about what happens to it between the factory and the shelf.

On your own

Ordering direct

  • Pieces nested without dividers, so chips are discovered on unpacking
  • No food-contact report, which retailers and customs can both ask for
  • Glaze and colour variation between batches nobody checked
  • Cartons too thin for the weight, failing at the bottom of the pallet
  • Replacement pieces unavailable because the set was never open stock
Through OZ

Buying through OZ

  • Divider pads and double-walled cartons specified, with drop testing at volume
  • LFGB or FDA test report arranged for the market you are selling into
  • A sealed approved sample held as the reference for the bulk run
  • Carton weight and stacking checked before the pallet is built
  • Open stock ordered alongside the sets, so replacements stay possible
Common questions

Before you send
the first enquiry.

These are the questions that decide whether a tableware order is sellable when it lands.

Is the tableware food-safe?

It can be, and for most markets it needs to be tested rather than assumed. We arrange LFGB or FDA food-contact testing on the actual production glaze and body, and we send you the report. If you are selling through a retailer, they will usually ask for it, so it is far cheaper to arrange before production than after.

Is it dishwasher and microwave safe?

Not automatically. Dishwasher and microwave safety depend on the glaze, the body and any decoration, especially metallic rims or decals. Tell us which you need and we will confirm it with the factory and mark it on the specification, rather than letting you find out at home.

How much breakage should I expect?

With proper dividers, double-walled cartons and sensible pallet stacking, breakage should be close to zero. Almost all the breakage we see comes from cost-cutting on packing, where a small saving per carton costs a replacement shipment. We photograph the packing before dispatch so you can see what was done.

Can I add my own logo or design?

Yes. Decals, printed logos and custom glazes are common, and they usually carry a setup cost plus a higher minimum quantity, typically three hundred pieces or more. If you are testing a design, buy plain stock first and add decoration once the shape has sold.
