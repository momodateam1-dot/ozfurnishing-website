Materials and construction

What it is made of
and what the label hides.

In bedding the fibre, the weave and the finish decide how a product performs after washing — which is exactly where cheap listings stop being cheap.

01

Cotton

Combed, carded and long-staple cotton, woven as percale or sateen. Ask for staple length and yarn count, not just thread count: a 400-count sheet from short-staple fibre pills.

↗
02

Lyocell & bamboo viscose

Cool, drapey and marketed hard. Both are regenerated cellulose, so check the mill's fibre supplier — quality and price vary far more than the labels suggest.

↗
03

Microfibre & polyester

Cheapest per set and the most consistent in bulk. Less breathable than natural fibre, which matters in warm climates and for anyone who sleeps hot.

↗
04

Linen & blends

Washed linen and linen-cotton blends. Premium look and genuinely breathable, but higher unit cost and it creases by design — which some buyers read as a defect.

↗
05

Down & feather fill

Fill power, down-cluster percentage and source certification are three separate numbers. All three should be on the spec sheet before you commit to a price.

↗
06

Silk

Mulberry silk in momme weights, mostly for pillowcases and eye masks. Verify momme weight and whether it is real silk or satin-weave polyester.

Buying a listing vs. buying a spec

What changes
when the fabric is written down.

Two sheet sets can photograph identically and differ by three years of service life. The difference is in the spec, not the picture.

On your own

Ordering direct

  • Thread count is the only number on the listing, and it is the easiest one to inflate
  • Fibre content and yarn count unverified until the fabric is in your hands
  • Colour and size drift between batches, so reorders rarely match the first
  • Shrinkage and pilling discovered at home, after the return window has closed
  • No certification paperwork, which blocks retail and marketplace listing
Through OZ

Buying through OZ

  • Fibre, yarn count, weave and finish written into the order before production
  • A sample washed and measured for shrinkage before bulk is confirmed
  • Approved sample held in the warehouse as the reference for reorders
  • Batch-to-batch colour checked against the approved piece, not against a photo
  • OEKO-TEX and RDS documentation requested with the order, not after it
Common questions

Before you send
the first enquiry.

These come up on nearly every bedding order.

Can I buy just a few sets?

Yes. Stocked bedding can usually be bought two to ten sets at a time, and there is no minimum order value. Freight is the thing to watch: duvets and pillows are bulky, so we compress and pack them to cut volume before quoting the shipment.

What thread count should I ask for?

Thread count alone is a weak signal. A 300-count sheet made from long-staple combed cotton will outlast a 1,000-count sheet made from short-staple fibre with multi-ply yarn. Tell us your target price and who will use the product, and we will propose a fibre, weave and count combination that makes sense for that use.

Do you wash-test samples?

Yes, and we recommend it. We measure the sample before washing, run a standard wash and dry cycle, then measure again and report the shrinkage and any colour change. It adds a few days and it is the single most useful number on a bedding order.

Can you do private label and custom packaging?

Yes. Woven labels, care labels, hangtags and retail packaging are all available, usually from one hundred units upward depending on the item. Send your artwork or a photo of the packaging you want to match and we will confirm what the mill needs.
